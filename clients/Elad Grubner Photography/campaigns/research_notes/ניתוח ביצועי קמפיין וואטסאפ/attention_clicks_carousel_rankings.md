Source-type labels used below: **[Official]** = Meta documentation; **[Independent]** = practitioner/trade press not selling a tool; **[Vendor]** = blog of a company selling an ads/analytics tool or agency service (treat numbers as marketing material). Research date: 26 Oct 2026.

Method note: Meta Business Help Center pages (facebook.com/business/help/...) render with JavaScript and could not be fetched directly; their wording below comes from search-engine extracts of those pages and is marked "(via search extract)". Jon Loomer's site returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; his wording comes from the search index of the page. The Meta for Developers Insights *breakdowns* page was fetched directly. ## 1. What exactly counts in Clicks (all), Link clicks, Outbound clicks and Unique link clicks, for a click-to-WhatsApp carousel ad

### Takeaway
Clicks (all) is a catch-all engagement count (link clicks + reactions/comments/shares + clicks to the Page/Instagram profile + expanding media to full screen + "see more"); Link clicks is the subset of taps on links to a chosen destination and explicitly includes click-to-message CTAs, so a tap on "Send WhatsApp message" is a link click; Outbound clicks only counts clicks that leave Meta (website, app store, deep link) and is therefore NOT the right metric for click-to-WhatsApp. No source (official or otherwise) confirms whether swiping between carousel cards counts toward Clicks (all).

### Cited Findings
- [Official] Meta's "Clicks (All)" help page: the metric "counts multiple types of clicks on your ad, including certain types of interactions with the ad container, links to other destinations, and links to expanded ad experiences." Listed inclusions: link clicks, clicks to the associated Facebook Page or Instagram profile, post reactions, comments and shares, clicks that expand media to full screen, and clicks that complete the campaign objective (via search extract) — [Meta Business Help Center: Clicks (All)](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787506997938504)
- [Official] Meta also keeps a dedicated troubleshooting page "Troubleshoot the Difference Between Link Clicks and Outbound Clicks" (page exists; content not retrievable) — [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/102928543574299)
- [Independent] Jon Loomer: Link clicks include "clicks, taps, or swipes on links to websites, app stores or app deep links, click-to-message CTAs, Shops, lead forms, Marketplace, playable experiences, and videos" (Meta's list as reproduced by Loomer; via search index). Outbound clicks only include clicks to websites, your app in an app store, and app deep links. Typical size order: Link clicks > Outbound clicks > Landing page views; LPV requires the Pixel — [Jon Loomer, "Are you using Link Clicks wrong?"](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/are-you-using-link-clicks-wrong/)
- [Vendor] Reportei, attributing to Meta: "Clicks counts all clicks, including link clicks and other interactions with an ad, such as liking or commenting." / "Link clicks counts clicks on links to selected destinations or experiences, on or off Meta's platforms." Examples of non-link clicks: Page name, likes/comments/shares, Page/profile clicks, expand photo/video to full screen. Article undated — [Reportei](https://reportei.com/en/?p=83838)
- [Vendor/agency] Attention Experts: Clicks (all) "is your broadest metric", including likes, shares, profile visits and "see more" clicks; Link clicks "only counts clicks to specific destinations like your landing page or Messenger"; Outbound clicks is the "pure" traffic metric that excludes internal Meta clicks; Unique link clicks counts people (one person clicking 5 times = 5 link clicks, 1 unique link click). Undated — [Attention Experts](https://attentionexperts.com/choosing-the-right-clicks-metric-in-meta-ads/)
- [Vendor] Motion glossary: link clicks include any click on a link in the ad, including some internal Meta destinations; outbound clicks specifically mean someone left Meta — [Motion glossary: Click](https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/click)
- [Vendor] SoCast: Clicks (All) includes likes, shares, comments; Clicks (Links) counts only link engagement — [SoCast support](https://support.socastdigital.com/portal/en/kb/articles/what-is-the-difference-between-clicks-all-and-clicks-links-in-socast-reach-campaign-analytics)
- Carousel swipes: none of the retrieved official or third-party definitions mentions carousel card navigation (swiping to the next card) as part of Clicks (all) or Link clicks. The official Clicks (All) list retrieved does not include it — [Meta Business Help Center: Clicks (All)](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787506997938504) (via search extract). Loomer's list mentions "swipes on links", which refers to swiping on a link/CTA, not card-to-card navigation — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/are-you-using-link-clicks-wrong/)
- Related: Meta reworded its *click-through attribution* definition in late Sept/early Oct 2025 to: "Counts results after any click occurred on your ad within 1-day or 7-days of an optimized conversion" and "Clicks may include interactions such as likes, shares and saves." Vaizle says behavior did not change, only wording; recommends comparing reported conversions to Link clicks / Outbound clicks / LPV. [Vendor/news, 13 Oct 2025] — [Vaizle Insights](https://insights.vaizle.com/news/meta-updates-click-attribution-definition/). CONFLICT: other secondary sources report a later change in which a "click" for attribution now means link clicks only and non-link interactions moved to a renamed "engage-through attribution" with a 1-day window (not fetched; reported via search summary) — [AdsUploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-click-attribution); [Avenue Z](https://avenuez.com/blog/metas-new-definition-of-click/). Both concern attribution windows, not the Clicks (all) / Link clicks reporting columns.
- What happens after the link click in click-to-WhatsApp: third-party CTWA platforms note Meta-reported ad clicks normally exceed chats actually started (illustrative example: 500 clicks vs 320 chats) [Vendor] — [DoubleTick](https://learn.doubletick.io/why-do-i-see-a-difference-between-meta-ad-clicks-and-chats-initiated-on-doubletick). Sudonum measured ~60 messages sent per 100 WhatsApp-button clicks on marketplace listings (website buttons, not Meta ads), better on mobile-only traffic [Vendor] — [Sudonum](https://www.sudonum.com/blog/sudonums-whatsapp-data)
- "Messaging conversations started" is described by a third party as counting a new chat started by a user after clicking the ad, with a repeat message within 7 days of the last one not counting as a new conversation [Vendor] — [reach.tools](https://reach.tools/?p=3337)

### Inferences
- For Elad's campaign the funnel has three measurable steps, and the current tool only sees the first one: (1) Clicks (all) = any interaction; (2) Link clicks = taps on the WhatsApp CTA/card link (the "intent" click); (3) Messaging conversations started = the person actually pressed Send in WhatsApp. The 108 → 2 drop (1.9%) cannot be diagnosed until step 2 is pulled. The relevant API fields are `inline_link_clicks`, `unique_inline_link_clicks`, `inline_link_click_ctr`, `cost_per_inline_link_click` (standard Insights field names; the fetch of their official field-reference page failed, so names should be confirmed against the API version the tool uses).
- Outbound clicks should be expected to be ~0 or empty for a click-to-WhatsApp ad (WhatsApp is not a website/app-store/deep-link destination per Loomer's list). The analyst should not use Outbound clicks or Landing page views for this campaign.
- Because carousel swipes are not documented as clicks, a high CTR (all) on a carousel should not be read as "people browsed the cards"; it may equally be reactions, "see more" on the Hebrew caption, profile taps, or full-screen photo expands. This is the core reason link clicks must be added.
- The Facebook-feed vs Instagram-feed CTR (all) gap (3.87% vs 1.43%) is also uninterpretable without link clicks: Facebook feed offers more non-link click targets (Page name, "see more", full-screen photo expand), so a higher CTR (all) there does not prove higher WhatsApp intent.
- The step from link click to conversation started is where pre-filled message friction, WhatsApp not installed/desktop sessions, and "just curious" taps show up; the vendor figures above (~60-64% message completion) are the only numbers found and come from different contexts, so they should not be used as a benchmark, only as evidence that a meaningful gap is normal.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve Meta's own verbatim Link clicks definition page or the Insights field-reference page (403/404/JS rendering). The click-to-message inclusion rests on Loomer's reproduction of Meta's list.
- No source confirms or denies that carousel card swipes count toward Clicks (all). This should be tested empirically (compare Clicks (all) vs link clicks + reactions + comments + shares + post saves + profile clicks per ad).
- Meta's official definition of "Messaging conversations started" (including the exact 7-day rule and attribution window) was not retrieved from Meta directly.
- No published, sourced benchmark for "link clicks → WhatsApp conversations started" for Meta click-to-WhatsApp ads was found.

## 2. How to read the ratio link clicks / all clicks, link CTR and cost per link click as diagnostics

### Takeaway
Practitioners treat Clicks (all)/CTR (all) as an attention/"did it stop the scroll" signal and Link clicks/CTR (link) as the intent signal; the gap between them is non-link engagement (likes, profile, "see more", expanding photos). No expert source found publishes a numeric "healthy" link-to-all ratio, so the ratio should be used comparatively (between ads, placements and days within this account), not against an external benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- [Vendor/agency] Clicks (all) is "best used for Top-of-Funnel (TOFU) awareness" and measures whether the creative stops the scroll; Link clicks is "a great indicator of Middle-of-Funnel (MOFU) engagement"; Unique link clicks shows whether you reach fresh people or the same small group clicking repeatedly — [Attention Experts](https://attentionexperts.com/choosing-the-right-clicks-metric-in-meta-ads/)
- [Independent] Loomer's argument is that advertisers misread Link clicks as "visits"; the metric to use depends on the destination (for website goals, outbound clicks or landing page views) — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/are-you-using-link-clicks-wrong/)
- [Vendor] Search-summarised glossary: "Clicks (all) is a measure of total engagement, not total intent"; CTR (all) = Clicks (all) / impressions — [Mockingbird](https://www.mockingbirdhouse.com/learning/metrics-and-attribution/meta-ad-metrics-explained) (via search summary, not fetched)
- [Official, 2019] Engagement rate ranking is built on expected engagement "including likes, reactions, comments, shares, and clicks anywhere on the ad", i.e. Meta itself treats clicks (all)-type interactions as the engagement layer, separate from conversion — [Social Media Examiner, 20 May 2019](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/facebook-ad-relevance-score-updates-what-marketers-need-to-know/)
- [Vendor/news] When comparing reported conversions to clicks, high conversions with low link clicks suggests engagement-based (non-link) attribution — [Vaizle, 13 Oct 2025](https://insights.vaizle.com/news/meta-updates-click-attribution-definition/)
- [Independent, trade press] Example of the misreading: an advertiser saw 1,500 ad clicks but only 150 site visitors in analytics (search-summarised, specific article not identified among results) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1180179) (note: context/date unclear; treat as anecdote)

### Inferences
- Diagnostic reading for this campaign (once link clicks are pulled), per ad and per placement:
  - **High CTR (all), low link-click share** → the photos attract attention (expanding, profile taps, "see more", reactions) but people do not tap the WhatsApp CTA: the issue is the offer/CTA/reason to message, not the creative's stopping power.
  - **Healthy link clicks, very few conversations started** → people intend to message but drop in WhatsApp: check the pre-filled message, the greeting/auto-reply, whether the ad set up the expectation of what happens next, and device mix (desktop Facebook feed vs mobile).
  - **Low CTR (all) and low link CTR** → the creative itself is not stopping the target audience (attention problem); this is where image choice/first card matters.
  - **Link clicks >> unique link clicks** → the same few people are clicking repeatedly (relevant for the ~3,000-3,500 warm audience, where frequency will rise fast).
- Cost per link click (spend / link clicks) is a cleaner "price of intent" than cost per click (all) (~2.63 ILS here), because cost per (all) click is deflated by non-intent interactions. For a messages-objective campaign, the decision metric remains cost per conversation started; cost per link click and link-click-to-conversation rate explain *where* that cost comes from.
- The link/all ratio should be compared between the 8 ads, between FB and IG feeds, and between the warm and Advantage+ ad sets, not against an internet benchmark (none credible was found).

### Gaps
- No credible source (official or named expert such as Loomer, Foxwell, AdEspresso, Social Media Examiner) gave a numeric threshold for the link-clicks / all-clicks ratio or a benchmark link CTR for click-to-WhatsApp ads; any such number would be invented.
- No Israeli/Hebrew-language professional source on this distinction was found (Hebrew search returned only English results).

## 3. Analysing carousel ads card by card

### Takeaway
Meta does expose per-card data via the "Carousel card" action breakdown (Ads Manager: Breakdown > By action > Carousel card; API: `action_carousel_card_id` / `action_carousel_card_name`), but it is an *action* breakdown: it reliably gives link clicks per card (and the API doc says on-Meta metrics like impressions and link clicks remain supported), cards are identified by their headline, and third-party connectors report link clicks as the only per-card metric. Card 1 is what shows before any interaction, and the "automatically show best-performing cards first" option reorders cards, which makes card-position analysis unreliable when it is on.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] `action_carousel_card_id`: "The ID of the specific carousel card that people engaged with when they saw your ad." `action_carousel_card_name`: the card people engaged with; "The cards are identified by their headlines." — [Meta for Developers: Insights API Breakdowns](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/insights/breakdowns/)
- [Official] Allowed combinations: card breakdown alone, or with `impression_device`, `country`, `age`, `gender`, or `age, gender`. These rows are not marked as combinable with `action_type` / `action_target_id` / `action_destination`. "Due to storage constraints, only some permutations of breakdowns are available." — [Meta for Developers: Insights API Breakdowns](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/insights/breakdowns/)
- [Official] The carousel card breakdowns are on the list affected by the 2021 off-Meta changes: off-Meta web metrics are returned without the breakdown value, mobile app metrics are no longer returned with these breakdowns, but the breakdowns "will still be supported for on-Meta metrics such as impressions and link clicks." Data before 27 April 2021 unaffected. The breakdowns are not marked deprecated — [Meta for Developers: Insights API Breakdowns](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/insights/breakdowns/)
- [Vendor] Ads Manager path: Breakdown > By action > Carousel card — [Netpeak](https://netpeak.us/blog/a-guide-to-posting-perfect-facebook-carousel-ads/) (via search summary)
- [Vendor] Funnel.io's connector: the carousel card breakdown "only supports one metric (Link Clicks), for each card in a carousel" (via search summary; page 404 on direct fetch) — [Funnel.io product update](https://funnel.io/product-updates/facebook-ads-carousel-card-breakdown)
- [Anecdotal] A Shopify community user reports the breakdown returning only whole-carousel stats with individual cards blank; no confirmed fix in thread — [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-to-track-individual-card-performance-in-facebook-carousel-ads/244290)
- [Vendor] "Automatically show best performing cards first" is a toggle that reorders cards by performance; advised on for independent-product carousels, off when a fixed sequence/story matters — [AdsUploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-carousel-ads); [Gethookd](https://www.gethookd.ai/learn/how-to-create-facebook-carousel-ads-templates-specs-tools/) (via search summary)
- [Vendor] Ranking logic is described inconsistently: per-card CTR (citing Meta's carousel API docs, unlinked) — [RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/carousel-ads-mobile-apps/); per-user ordering using engagement and browsing signals — [Akohub](https://akohub.com/help/can-i-change-the-order-of-the-carousel). Exact criterion unconfirmed.
- [Vendor] Card 1 is what appears in the feed before anyone engages — [RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/carousel-ads-mobile-apps/)
- [Trade press, 2014 — dated] Kinetic Social data reported carousel CTR of ~1% vs ~0.1% for non-carousel ads; very old, pre-dates current formats; not a benchmark — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/facebooks-carousel-ads-10-times-better-regular-ads/)
- [Vendor, LinkedIn not Meta] Card-position data on LinkedIn carousels: Card 1 had a 76.32% impression share (44-ad sample) — illustrates the first-card concentration but is a different platform — [ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-ads-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- For Elad's 8 carousels (one project per carousel, several photos each), a per-card read should be: link clicks per card (from the Carousel card breakdown), share of the ad's link clicks per card, and — if the breakdown returns it — impressions per card. Because card 1 is the only card everyone sees, raw link clicks will be concentrated on card 1 almost by construction; a later card that still gets link clicks is a strong signal that the photo pulls interest.
- If "show best-performing cards first" is on, card order differs per viewer, so "card 1 vs card 3" comparisons are not position comparisons; the analyst must first check this setting in the plan/ad before interpreting card position. If off, card 1 performance is effectively the ad's "hook".
- Since cards are identified by headline, carousels where all cards share the same headline (or have none) may be indistinguishable in `action_carousel_card_name`; use `action_carousel_card_id` and map IDs to the photo files in the campaign folder.
- With ~13 all-clicks per ad on average (108 / 8 ads), per-card link clicks will be single digits or zero for most cards on day 5; card-level conclusions are not possible yet, only a check that the data flows.

### Gaps
- No official Meta document listing exactly which metrics are returned per card (impressions? reach? messaging conversations started?) was retrieved. Whether `onsite_conversion.messaging_conversation_started_7d` can be split by carousel card is unknown and must be tested with a read-only query.
- No authoritative source documents the exact ranking criterion of "show best-performing cards first", or whether it can be detected via the API on an existing ad.
- No named-expert (Loomer, Foxwell, AdEspresso) guidance on card-by-card analysis was retrieved within the search budget.

## 4. Ad relevance diagnostics: quality ranking, engagement rate ranking, conversion rate ranking

### Takeaway
The three rankings (introduced 2019, replacing the 1-10 relevance score) still appear as ad-level columns in Ads Manager per 2025-2026 secondary sources; no deprecation was found. They compare an ad with ads competing for the same audience, need roughly 500+ impressions to show, are not auction inputs, and are best used only to localise a problem (creative quality vs attention vs post-click). For this campaign most ads are likely near or below the 500-impression floor, so blanks are expected and should not be read as "bad".

### Cited Findings
- [Trade press, 2019] Facebook retired the single relevance score and replaced it with quality ranking, engagement rate ranking and conversion rate ranking — [MarTech](https://martech.org/facebook-to-replace-relevance-score-with-3-new-metrics-in-april); old score removed 30 April 2019 — [Newsfeed.org](https://newsfeed.org/?p=16991)
- [Trade press, 2019] Definitions as described at launch: Quality ranking = perceived ad quality measured from feedback on ads and the post-click experience; Engagement rate ranking = expected engagement rate, "including likes, reactions, comments, shares, and clicks anywhere on the ad"; Conversion rate ranking = expected conversion rate vs ads with the same optimisation goal competing for the same audience. Buckets: Below Average / Average / Above Average. Requires more than 500 impressions. Rankings are estimates "to be used as a guide"; a low score doesn't mean the ad gets no results; Facebook advised that moving from below average to average matters more than average to above average; diagnostics are per ad, not per ad set/campaign — [Social Media Examiner, 20 May 2019](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/facebook-ad-relevance-score-updates-what-marketers-need-to-know/)
- [Trade press, 2019] Interpretation guidance: Quality below average while others average+ → change image/copy or audience; Engagement below average → make the ad more eye-catching or target a more engaged audience; Conversion below average → improve CTA / post-click experience or targeting — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/facebook-ad-relevance-score-updates-what-marketers-need-to-know/)
- [Official claim via trade press] Diagnostics are not used in auction pricing; meant to guide creative, targeting and post-click changes — [LocaliQ UK](https://localiq.co.uk/1057); 500-impression threshold — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/facebook-replaces-relevance-score-with-a-new-ad-relevance-diagnostics/)
- [Agency blog, dated 26 Oct 2026, attributing to Meta without links] Buckets: Above average (above ~55th percentile), Average (~35th-55th), Below average split into bottom 35%, bottom 20%, bottom 10%. ~500 impressions needed. Quality ranking only available for the last 35 days; not available for dynamic creative; columns blank where a ranking doesn't apply; short date ranges and low-budget ads often show no value; Meta states diagnostics are not an auction ranking factor. Columns found via Ads tab > Customise columns > search "ranking". Combination reading (author's own): quality low only → misleading copy/landing; engagement low only → creative not stopping this audience; conversion low only → post-click friction/offer mismatch/wrong event; all three low → ad and audience mismatched — [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/facebook-ads/meta-ads-relevance-diagnostics-quality-engagement-conversion-ranking/)
- [Vendor, 2025] Lists relevance diagnostics among current Meta Ads reporting metrics — [MB Adv](https://www.mbadv.agency/meta-ads/meta-ads-metrics-reporting-and-optimization) (via search summary)
- CONFLICT on auction impact: Meta (via LocaliQ 2019, Relevant Audience 2026) says diagnostics are not auction inputs; at least one secondary source in search results claimed relevance signals feed the auction's quality component and another claimed "25-60% lower CPM" for above-average ads — both unverified and should be ignored — [search summary of adwize/adriselab/clikim pages, not fetched](https://adwize.ai/blog/meta-ad-relevance-diagnostics)
- Search for 2025-2026 removal news found none; results about "removal" all refer to the 2019 retirement of the old relevance score — [MarTech](https://martech.org/facebook-to-replace-relevance-score-with-3-new-metrics-in-april)

### Inferences
- In this campaign, 3,758 impressions spread over 8 ads ≈ 470 impressions per ad on average (my arithmetic; actual split is uneven, the 50 ILS/day Advantage+ ad set likely holds more). Several ads, especially in the 25 ILS/day warm ad set, will be below the ~500 floor, so blank rankings are expected; the analyst must report "not enough data" rather than infer anything.
- Engagement rate ranking is the closest Meta-provided proxy for the "attention" layer of a static/carousel ad (it explicitly includes clicks anywhere on the ad). Conversion rate ranking compares against ads with the same optimisation goal (here: conversations), so it is the ranking that speaks to the "click → WhatsApp message" step.
- The warm audience (~3,000-3,500 people) is compared against other ads competing for *that* audience; rankings in a tiny B2B niche may be volatile and based on few competitors. Use them only as a "where to look" hint, never as a stop/scale signal; cost per conversation remains the decision metric.
- A combination that would be meaningful for Elad once ads cross 500 impressions: engagement ranking average/above + conversion ranking below average = photos work, the WhatsApp step/offer does not (consistent with "108 clicks, 2 conversations").

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve Meta's current help page text ("About ad relevance diagnostics", business/help/403110480493160) to confirm 2026 status, percentile buckets, the 35-day rule, or whether conversion rate ranking is computed for the Messages/conversations goal.
- Whether the rankings are available via the Insights API for this account's API version (`quality_ranking`, `engagement_rate_ranking`, `conversion_rate_ranking`) was not confirmed from an official page in this session.
- No 2024-2026 expert commentary (Loomer, Foxwell, AdEspresso) on how much weight to give rankings was retrieved.

## 5. Other attention signals for static/carousel ads

### Takeaway
For static and carousel ads the attention layer is read from engagement actions (reactions, comments, shares, saves, profile/Page clicks, "see more", full-screen expands — all folded into Clicks (all)) and from engagement rate ranking. Hook rate / thumb-stop rate is a video metric (3-second plays / impressions) and does not apply to Elad's photo carousels.

### Cited Findings
- [Vendor] Thumbstop rate / hook rate = 3-second video plays ÷ impressions × 100; Motion's glossary calls 20-40% generally solid; Motion's blog treats <25% as needing work and >35% as high — [Motion glossary](https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/thumbstop-rate); some vendors instead define hook rate on total video plays rather than impressions — [Segwise glossary](https://segwise.ai/glossary/hook-rate)
- [Official, 2019] Meta's own engagement construct = likes, reactions, comments, shares, and clicks anywhere on the ad — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/facebook-ad-relevance-score-updates-what-marketers-need-to-know/)
- [Official via search extract] Clicks (all) includes Page/profile clicks, reactions/comments/shares and full-screen media expands — [Meta Business Help Center: Clicks (All)](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/787506997938504)
- [Vendor/news] Meta's 2025 attribution wording names likes, shares and saves as "soft interactions" that can count as clicks for attribution — [Vaizle](https://insights.vaizle.com/news/meta-updates-click-attribution-definition/)
- [Vendor] A per-metric reference of Meta post engagement metrics and their API fields exists (not fetched) — [Affect Group](https://affectgroup.com/blog/meta-ads-post-engagement-metrics-definitions-formulas-and-api-fields/)

### Inferences
- For the AI analyst, the "attention" panel for each carousel ad should decompose Clicks (all) into: link clicks, post reactions, comments, shares, saves, and the residual (profile clicks, see more, photo expands, possibly swipes — unverifiable). Saves and shares are particularly relevant for a B2B photography audience (designers saving reference images) and may be a positive interest signal even when they don't convert to WhatsApp that week.
- Comments in a niche B2B audience should be read qualitatively (who commented — designer/architect or not), not as a rate.
- Do not report hook rate or thumb-stop rate for these ads; they are undefined for static/carousel images.

### Gaps
- No source quantified the share of Clicks (all) typically made up of non-link interactions for carousel ads.
- No source documented a "card swipe" or "carousel engagement depth" metric in Meta reporting.

## 6. What a professional creative-level read looks like on small numbers

### Takeaway
Sourced guidance is thin and mostly vendor-made; the consistent message is: decide on conversions (not impressions or CTR), avoid peeking at early data, run at least ~7 days, and expect to need dozens of conversions per variant to tell ads apart. At Elad's scale (2 conversations total, ~13 all-clicks per ad), creative-level comparisons on conversions are impossible for weeks; only large CTR gaps on attention metrics can be read, and even those carry wide uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- [Vendor] About 50 conversions per variant to detect a 20% performance difference at 95% confidence; ~200 for 10%; 800+ for 5% (CreativeOS figures reported by Segwise) — [Segwise, 2026 guide](https://segwise.ai/blog/ad-creative-testing-best-practices)
- [Vendor citing Meta] Meta's rule of thumb: optimise toward an event that happens around 50 times per week — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/ad-creative-testing-best-practices)
- [Vendor] Run tests at least 7 days to cover day-of-week variation; deciding on early data before significance is a common mistake — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/ad-creative-testing-best-practices)
- [Vendor] One creative-testing platform requires every variant to exceed 100 impressions before declaring a winner — [Luna Labs knowledge base](https://knowledge.lunalabs.io/en/articles/7156686-what-is-the-process-for-selecting-a-winner-in-a-creative-test)
- [Unverified] A self-published methodology suggests 800-2,200 impressions per creative in 24-48 hours, with no stated statistical basis — [Notion: Creative Testing System](https://handy-moat-a06.notion.site/THE-CREATIVE-TESTING-SYSTEM-CTS-2ba2f6dd530880e88b26d23cb805b702)
- [Vendor] Small-budget creative testing guide exists (not fetched) — [GrowthMentor](https://www.growthmentor.com/blog/creative-testing-with-small-ads-budget)
- [Official/agency] Relevance diagnostics show nothing below ~500 impressions per ad — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/facebook-replaces-relevance-score-with-a-new-ad-relevance-diagnostics/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/facebook-ads/meta-ads-relevance-diagnostics-quality-engagement-conversion-ranking/)

### Inferences
- My own calculations (Wilson 95% intervals, not from a source) show how wide the uncertainty is at this scale:
  - Campaign CTR (all) 108/3,758 = 2.87% → plausible range 2.39%-3.46%.
  - An ad with 20 clicks on 500 impressions (4.0%) → 2.6%-6.1%; an ad with 8 clicks on 500 impressions (1.6%) → 0.8%-3.1%. The ranges almost touch, so even the best vs worst ad in the 1.59%-4.03% spread is only borderline distinguishable if each ad has ~500 impressions.
  - At 1,000 impressions each, 4.0% (2.95%-5.4%) vs 1.6% (0.99%-2.58%) become clearly separable — i.e., roughly 1,000 impressions per ad is where a 2.5x CTR gap stops being noise.
  - Click-to-conversation rate 2/108 = 1.9% → plausible range 0.5%-6.5%; two conversations cannot establish a rate, only flag a possible problem worth checking with link clicks.
- Practical rules for the AI analyst (derived from the cited guidance + the arithmetic, not from a named expert): (a) do not compare ads on conversions until each has a meaningful count (dozens, per the 50-per-variant guidance — unreachable for this budget, so compare at ad-set or project level instead); (b) compare ads on CTR (all)/link CTR only when each has ~1,000 impressions and the gap is large (≥2x); (c) always show the count behind a rate (e.g., "4.03% = 19 clicks"), never a bare percentage; (d) report relevance rankings only for ads past 500 impressions; (e) never call a creative winner/loser on a single day's data.
- With 75 ILS/day, Meta's ~50 optimisation events/week rule of thumb is far out of reach (2 conversations in 5 days), so the campaign will stay data-poor; aggregated reads (all 4 carousels of a project across both ad sets, or the whole ad set) are more trustworthy than per-ad reads.
