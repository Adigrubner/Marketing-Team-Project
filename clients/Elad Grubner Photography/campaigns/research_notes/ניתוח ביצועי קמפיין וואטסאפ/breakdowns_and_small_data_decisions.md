## 1. Meta's "breakdown effect" - how to read placement / age / gender breakdowns, and when exclusion IS justified

### Takeaway
Meta's delivery system spends where the NEXT result is cheapest, not where the AVERAGE looks cheapest, so a segment (placement, age band) with a higher average cost per result is often part of what keeps the whole ad set cheap; cutting it on average-cost grounds usually raises total cost. Exclusion is justified only when a segment produces cheap but low-quality "results" (classic case: Audience Network clicks/ThruPlays) or takes a large share of spend with zero real business outcomes over a meaningful sample - and even then experts prefer bid adjustments (value rules) over removal.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Meta has a dedicated Help Center article titled "About the 'breakdown effect' when advertising on our system" (facebook.com/business/help/770303663944673). Its body could not be retrieved here (JS-rendered; only the title returned) - [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/770303663944673)
- [OFFICIAL via PRACTITIONER] Meta's documented example (as described by Loomer): $500 budget, two placements. Early on Facebook Stories "significantly outperforms" Instagram Stories; on Day 4 Instagram Stories starts outperforming; Meta shifts roughly $450 to Instagram Stories and ~$50 to Facebook Stories; heavier spend raises Instagram Stories' costs so its overall AVERAGE CPA ends up looking worse - which advertisers misread as misallocation. Meta calls this misreading the "breakdown effect"; the danger is that advertisers override automation. Article dated 19 May 2025 - [Jon Loomer, Meta's Breakdown Effect](https://www.jonloomer.com/breakdown-effect/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Loomer's rules for avoiding wrong conclusions: (a) look at the trend over time (daily breakdown), not the average of the whole period; (b) small audiences (e.g. remarketing) hit diminishing returns fast and forcing spend on them raises costs; (c) judge in aggregate - across the ad set for Advantage+ placements and for multiple ads, across the campaign for Advantage campaign budget; (d) avoid constant manual budget changes; (e) don't turn off an asset just because its average CPA looks higher - "turning it off often raises overall costs." Also quotes Meta that early results "are based on a small sample size and may not predict how things will perform over time" - [Jon Loomer, Breakdown Effect, May 2025](https://www.jonloomer.com/breakdown-effect/)
- [VENDOR, secondhand] AdvertiseMint attributes to Facebook the statement that the breakdown effect "occurs as the system's discount pacing intersects with its automation"; discount pacing starts with the cheapest available results and moves to more expensive ones as spend grows, so average cost per event rises with budget - [AdvertiseMint](https://www.advertisemint.com/?p=12224)
- [VENDOR] Kontra: if a placement has a lower average CPA but receives less budget, Meta was likely confident it had already captured the low-cost conversions available there; the signal to watch is rising MARGINAL CPA (cost over time within each placement) - [Kontra Agency](https://kontra.agency/what-stops-you-from-scaling-facebook-campaigns/)
- [PRACTITIONER] When removal IS justified: "Meta will exploit weaknesses in placements to get you cheaper actions" - Audience Network (cheap, likely low-quality clicks, accidental clicks, bots), Audience Network Rewarded Video (inflated ThruPlays - warning sign: more ThruPlays than people reached), Facebook Reels for Reach. Even then he suggests lowering the placement's bid with value rules instead of removing it. Article published 2 Dec 2025, updated 20 Jan 2026 - [Jon Loomer, 8 Essential Breakdowns](https://www.jonloomer.com/essential-breakdowns-meta-ads-manager/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Older but still-maintained Loomer rule (published 2019, modified 1 Mar 2025): leave placements alone unless there is a specific reason - e.g. "if Facebook is spending a large chunk of your budget on Audience Network and you aren't getting conversions, you may want to remove it"; otherwise let it ride, or duplicate into a new ad set/campaign with different placements - [Jon Loomer, edits and learning phase](https://www.jonloomer.com/facebook-ads-edits-learning-phase/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Placement controls are being restricted: in a test (article 20-24 Aug 2026) Meta is removing the ability to remove individual placements, remove whole platforms, limit to mobile-only/desktop-only, or limit by OS - per Loomer only for Sales and Leads objectives (other objectives keep full control; no date announced; his understanding, not an official announcement). Remaining levers: value rules (bid up/down by 7 placements, mobile vs desktop, mobile OS), account-level placement removal (Advertising Settings > Account Controls > Placement Controls), and the "excluded placements with up to 5% of budget" option added in the past year. He says most advertisers who remove placements don't need to, and removal makes sense when a placement yields cheap low-quality optimized actions - [Jon Loomer, Meta removing placement controls](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-removing-placement-controls-ad-sets/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Age/gender: "In general, I recommend sticking with age and gender defaults." Example: a lead-magnet campaign spent ~45% on 65+ and ~70% on 55+, many low-quality leads; a value rule cut 55+ spend share from 70% to 17% without excluding anyone. For gender: when optimizing for engagement, men may engage and Meta counts that as the wanted action; for purchase optimization he would not restrict to women only - [Jon Loomer, 8 Essential Breakdowns](https://www.jonloomer.com/essential-breakdowns-meta-ads-manager/)
- [VENDOR] Flighted: "Do not exclude demographics that look weak"; value rules are "the one legitimate override tool" for experienced buyers with a clear hypothesis, with a "4-week minimum evaluation window" - [Flighted](https://www.flighted.co/blog/the-meta-ads-breakdown-effect-explained)
- [VENDOR, AI skill file] A published Meta-ads analyzer skill instructs: treat a cut based on a high average CPA as a hypothesis to test, not an instruction; never recommend pausing a segment solely on higher average CPA - [Gooseworks meta-ads-analyzer skill](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/gooseworks-ai/goose-skills/meta-ads-analyzer)
- [VENDOR - definitional conflict] Ryze uses "breakdown effect" for something different (performance degradation from fatigue/audience exhaustion). This is NOT Meta's definition; ignore it - [Ryze](https://www.get-ryze.ai/blog/meta-ads-breakdown-effect-and-marginal-roas-analysis)

### Inferences
- Decision rule (breakdowns): never propose excluding a placement/age/gender from the average cost-per-result column alone. A proposal to exclude needs ALL of: (1) the segment took a large share of spend, (2) over a sample large enough to expect several results at target cost (see section 3), (3) zero or near-zero REAL outcomes in the owner's conversation log (not just Meta's count), and (4) a quality explanation (low-quality clicks, wrong people), not just a higher average.
- Applied to Elad, day 5: Facebook feed 164.57 ILS / 1 conversation vs Instagram feed 119.26 ILS / 1 conversation is a 1-vs-1 comparison - statistically indistinguishable (see section 3). The CTR gap (3.87% vs 1.43%) is about clicks, not conversations; with only 2 placements in use, both premium feed placements, there is no "low-quality placement" story here. Correct reading: "first sign only, no action." Exclusion would also be a targeting edit that resets learning (section 4).
- Age 45-54 taking ~90 of 283.83 ILS (~32%) is not by itself a problem for a professional B2B audience (experienced designers/architects). The right check is the conversation log: did the relevant designer fall in that band? Meta's age breakdown of spend is not a breakdown of relevance.
- CTR (all) counts all clicks, not only clicks that open WhatsApp; for a click-to-WhatsApp ad the meaningful per-placement metrics are cost per messaging conversation started and, above that, relevant conversations from the log. (Definition of CTR(all) not re-verified on Meta's page in this session - see Gaps.)

### Gaps
- Meta's own breakdown-effect article text (exact wording and any explicit "when to exclude" guidance) could not be retrieved; everything Meta-attributed is via Loomer/AdvertiseMint.
- Whether value rules are available for an Engagement/messages-objective campaign (vs Sales/Leads) was not confirmed.
- Exact Meta definition of "CTR (all)" for carousel ads (whether card swipes count) was not verified here.

## 2. Which breakdowns matter for a click-to-WhatsApp campaign (device, hour, gender) and how often to check them

### Takeaway
For click-to-WhatsApp the useful breakdowns are: placement (sanity check only), age and gender (compare with the real conversation log, not with average cost), device/platform (desktop users go through an extra WhatsApp Web step and convert less), and time (day trend; hour of day mainly to know when replies are needed, not to restrict delivery). Restricting hours or demographics usually hurts; Loomer tried dayparting and it did not help.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Loomer's 8 essential breakdowns (Dec 2025, updated Jan 2026): audience segments, placement, age/gender, country, dynamic creative element (format; text/headline), time (day/week/2 weeks/month), plus value rules. Device appears only via value rules and "conversion device"; he gives no device or hour-of-day advice - [Jon Loomer, 8 Essential Breakdowns](https://www.jonloomer.com/essential-breakdowns-meta-ads-manager/)
- [PRACTITIONER] The "Audience segments" breakdown (engaged audience / existing customers / new) requires Sales campaigns - so it is not available for a messages campaign. In one example ~13% of budget went to remarketing under fully broad targeting - [Jon Loomer, 8 Essential Breakdowns](https://www.jonloomer.com/essential-breakdowns-meta-ads-manager/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Time breakdown (day/week/month) is for trends; example: monthly CPM rose ~27% Sep-Nov but did not directly raise cost per result - [Jon Loomer, 8 Essential Breakdowns](https://www.jonloomer.com/essential-breakdowns-meta-ads-manager/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Dayparting: Loomer "did deep research on the days and times that led to the best cost per conversion, but leveraging this didn't help"; restricting hours usually restricts the algorithm and drives up costs - [Jon Loomer, should you run ads on a schedule](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/should-you-run-ads-on-a-schedule/)
- [VENDOR] Native ad scheduling (restricting hours) requires a lifetime budget; with daily budgets only "budget scheduling" (raising spend in high-demand periods) is available; sources conflict on whether the schedule uses the ad account's or the viewer's time zone - [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/meta-campaign-scheduling); [AdAdvisor](https://adadvisor.ai/blog/facebook-ads-scheduling)
- [VENDOR] Click-to-WhatsApp on desktop: the ad opens a WhatsApp web page with a "Continue to Chat" button; users without the app are prompted to download or log in to WhatsApp Web; on mobile users go straight into the chat - [bot.space CTWA guide](https://www.bot.space/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-click-to-whatsapp-ads-strategy-setup-and-optimization-for-2025)
- [VENDOR, anecdotal] A click-tracking operator reported ~60 messages per 100 WhatsApp-button clicks overall, "largely a Mobile vs Desktop issue", improving to ~75% on mobile only (marketplace listings, not ads specifically) - [Sudonum](https://www.sudonum.com/blog/sudonums-whatsapp-data)
- [VENDOR] Actions inside WhatsApp conversations cannot be tracked in Ads Manager - [360dialog](https://360dialog.com/blog/click-to-whatsapp-ads)
- [PRACTITIONER] Limiting to mobile-only/desktop-only is one of the controls Meta is testing removal of, but per Loomer only for Sales and Leads objectives; value rules can bid mobile vs desktop - [Jon Loomer, placement controls](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-removing-placement-controls-ad-sets/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Gender: under engagement-type optimization Meta may deliver to men who engage because it treats that as the wanted action; for purchase optimization he would not restrict to women - [Jon Loomer, 8 Essential Breakdowns](https://www.jonloomer.com/essential-breakdowns-meta-ads-manager/)
- [VENDOR] Value rules / demographic overrides need a 4-week minimum evaluation window - [Flighted](https://www.flighted.co/blog/the-meta-ads-breakdown-effect-explained)

### Inferences
- Breakdown checklist for this campaign (proposed rule set, not from a single source):
  - Placement (FB feed vs IG feed): weekly glance, judge by conversations and log relevance, never by CTR or average cost alone. Action only if one placement takes most spend for a sustained period with zero relevant conversations.
  - Device (impression device / platform): check once at ~500 ILS. If desktop takes a material share of spend with zero conversations, that is the one breakdown with a mechanical reason to underperform for WhatsApp (extra WhatsApp Web step). Lowest-cost fix candidate = value rule or mobile-only - both are edits (mobile-only is a targeting/placement change; see section 4).
  - Gender: check at milestones against the conversation log. A messages objective optimizes for "conversation started", so if men or non-designers start conversations the algorithm learns from them - the log is the ground truth.
  - Age: same as gender; compare relevant conversations by age with spend by age at 500 ILS, not daily.
  - Hour of day: use only to know when conversations arrive so replies are fast; do not propose dayparting (needs lifetime budget, and Loomer found it unhelpful).
  - Day trend: the default daily view - spend, conversations, cost per conversation, frequency in the warm ad set.
- Elad's warm audience (~3,000-3,500) has a mechanical reason to show rising frequency and rising cost per result as spend accumulates (Loomer's small-audience diminishing-returns point).

### Gaps
- No public benchmark for the mobile/desktop split of click-to-WhatsApp conversations; only one anecdotal operator figure.
- No source found on the gender split of Israeli interior designers/architects.
- Meta's official documentation for the time-of-day breakdown (which time zone, availability) was not retrieved.

## 3. Deciding with few conversions - small-sample rules, confidence intervals, Bayesian thinking

### Takeaway
With conversion counts of 0-5, single-day or single-segment comparisons are noise; the robust tools are (a) spend-multiple kill rules grounded in Poisson math (3x target cost with zero results is the common threshold, ~5% chance of being wrong), (b) exact Poisson / Wilson intervals instead of point estimates, and (c) deciding checkpoints in advance to avoid "peeking." Applied to Elad, the data already make it very unlikely that the true cost per relevant conversation is at or below 40 ILS, but it says almost nothing about which placement/ad/age is better.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR] Common kill rules: pause after ~2x target CPA with no conversions, or ~3x with none; 3x is the most widely cited starting kill rule - [AdBid automated rules guide 2026](https://adbid.me/blog/meta-ads-automated-rules-guide-2026); [Growwithba testing budget rules](https://growwithba.com/blog/meta-ads-testing-budget-rules); [Growwithba kill criteria](https://growwithba.com/blog/facebook-ads-kill-criteria)
- [VENDOR] Statistical basis: modelling conversions as Poisson, an ad truly on target that has spent 3x target CPA shows zero conversions only ~5% of the time - [AdManage, when to kill a Facebook ad](https://admanage.ai/blog/when-to-kill-a-facebook-ad). [CALC] Verified: P(0 | expected k) = e^-k -> 1x: 36.8%, 2x: 13.5%, 3x: 5.0%, 4x: 1.8%. So "2x" is a much weaker rule than "3x".
- [VENDOR] Read on a creative needs ~2-3x target CPA in spend, with a floor of ~$100-150 - [SparkUGC](https://www.sparkugc.com/resources/how-much-to-spend-per-creative-meta-ads-test)
- [VENDOR, counterpoint] One source argues the 2-3x CPA rule rests on statistically insignificant data and prefers LTV-based ceilings - [Holschuh](https://www.holschuh.co.uk/solved?id=solved-determining-minimum-daily-budget-for-facebook-ads)
- [STATS, independent] Evan Miller: "Repeated significance testing always increases the rate of false positives." In his example, stopping at the first p<0.05 (up to 150 observations) gives a 26.1% false-positive rate instead of 5%. To keep a true 5% level the per-peek threshold must tighten (1 peek 2.9%, 5 peeks 1.4%, 10 peeks 1.0%). Recommendations: fix sample size in advance; rule of thumb n = 16 sigma^2 / delta^2 (for a rate, sigma^2 = p(1-p)); or use sequential or Bayesian designs that allow valid inference at any time - [Evan Miller, How Not To Run an A/B Test](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-run-an-ab-test.html)
- [STATS] Rule of three: with zero events in n trials, the 95% upper bound on the rate is about 3/n - [Wikipedia, Rule of three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(statistics))
- [STATS] Wilson score interval for a proportion (use for CTR and click-to-conversation rate): centre = (p + z^2/2n)/(1 + z^2/n), half-width = z*sqrt(p(1-p)/n + z^2/4n^2)/(1 + z^2/n), z = 1.96 for 95% - [Wikipedia, Binomial proportion confidence interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_proportion_confidence_interval)
- [STATS] Exact Poisson interval for a count k: lower = chi2(alpha/2; 2k)/2, upper = chi2(1-alpha/2; 2k+2)/2 - [Wikipedia, Poisson distribution - confidence interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_distribution#Confidence_interval)
- [PRACTITIONER] Early results are small-sample and may not predict later performance (Meta wording quoted by Loomer) - [Jon Loomer, Breakdown Effect](https://www.jonloomer.com/breakdown-effect/)
- [CALC] Elad, day 5, relevant conversations (1 in 283.83 ILS), target 40 ILS: expected relevant conversations if truly on target = 283.83/40 = 7.1. P(1 or fewer | on target) = 0.67%; P(2 or fewer) = 2.8%. Exact 95% interval for cost per relevant conversation with k=1: 50.9 to ~11,200 ILS. Bayesian (Gamma-Poisson, Jeffreys prior): P(true cost per relevant conversation <= 40 ILS) ~ 0.3%. Method: Poisson/Wilson references above.
- [CALC] Elad, all Meta-counted conversations (2 in 283.83 ILS): 95% interval for cost per conversation 39.3 to ~1,172 ILS; P(true cost per conversation <= 40) ~ 1.4% (Jeffreys prior).
- [CALC] Exact 95% count intervals useful as a lookup (multiply into "spend / upper" and "spend / lower" for cost per result): k=0: 0-3.69; k=1: 0.025-5.57; k=2: 0.24-7.22; k=3: 0.62-8.77; k=5: 1.62-11.67; k=10: 4.80-18.39.
- [CALC] Power of the owner's 500 ILS rule ("2+ relevant = continue"): if the true cost per relevant conversation were 100 ILS, P(2+ by 500) = 96%; at 150 ILS = 84.5%; at 250 ILS = 59%. If it were truly 40 ILS, the expected count at 500 ILS is 12.5 and P(1 or fewer) = 0.005%.
- [CALC] Wilson illustration of CTR noise: 1 click-to-chat in 30 clicks -> 95% interval 0.6%-16.7%; 3 in 100 -> 1.0%-8.5%. With impressions in the thousands, a 3.87% vs 1.43% CTR gap would be statistically clear (e.g. 116/3000 -> 3.2%-4.6% vs 43/3000 -> 1.1%-1.9%; these impression counts are hypothetical - plug in real ones).

### Inferences
- "First sign" vs "pattern" rule for the analyst: call something a PATTERN only when the 95% intervals of the two things being compared do not overlap, or when a zero-result segment has spent >= 3x the target cost per result; otherwise label it "first sign, not enough data" and do not propose a change based on it. CTR differences become patterns quickly (thousands of impressions); conversation differences between placements/ages/ads almost never will at 75 ILS/day.
- The day-5 data are already enough to say, with high confidence, that the campaign is NOT running at <= 40 ILS per relevant conversation (assuming relevant conversations are reported fully and the rate is roughly stable). This is a statement about the overall level, not about which segment to cut.
- The 500 ILS rule tests "is this viable at all" (it passes a campaign running at ~250 ILS per relevant conversation more often than not), not "is it at 40 ILS". That is a business choice (one designer client may be worth far more than 40 ILS) - present both numbers to the owner, don't change the rule.
- Caveats on all [CALC] items: Poisson assumes a stable rate (learning-phase days are usually costlier), relevant-conversation counting depends on the owner's log and may lag, and Meta's conversation count is per conversation session.

### Gaps
- No named top practitioner (Foxwell, Heath, Mandalia, CTC) source was found in this session stating a specific spend-multiple rule; the 2x/3x rules come from vendor blogs (math verified independently).
- No expert source found that publishes a Bayesian calculator specific to Meta ads; the Bayesian figures above are my calculation using a standard conjugate model.

## 4. What counts as a "significant edit" (learning reset) in 2025-2026, and what to change first

### Takeaway
Meta's documented significant edits are: any targeting change, any creative change, changing the optimization event, adding a new ad, pausing the ad set 7+ days, and changing bid strategy; budget/bid amount changes count "depending on the magnitude" (Meta's only example: $100->$101 unlikely, $100->$1,000 may). The popular "20%" budget threshold is a community rule of thumb, not Meta's. Pausing a single ad, renaming, and (per Loomer) placement edits are not on Meta's list. The cheapest moves are those outside Meta (reply speed, conversation handling) or small budget moves; adding/changing ads or audiences resets learning for that ad set.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL via PRACTITIONER] Meta: "The learning phase occurs when you create a new ad or ad set or make a significant edit to an existing one." Edits that trigger it: any targeting change (even small age/country/interest tweaks and audience exclusions), any creative change (image, video, text, even typo fixes), optimization event change, adding a new ad to an existing ad set, pausing for 7 days or longer, switching to cost cap/bid cap/target cost. Magnitude-dependent ("roll of the dice"): spending limit, bid/cost/ROAS control amount, budget amount. Meta's example: "$100 to $101" not likely; "$100 to $1000" may. Loomer considers safe (Meta doesn't address): placements (but don't change without reason), names, URL parameters, pixel tracking, schedule. Published Dec 2019, modified 1 Mar 2025 - [Jon Loomer, edits and learning phase](https://www.jonloomer.com/facebook-ads-edits-learning-phase/)
- [OFFICIAL via VENDOR, checked 1 Oct 2026] Meta wording reproduced: "only a significant edit causes an ad set to re-enter the learning phase"; budget/bid/ROAS goal/spending-limit changes count depending on "the magnitude of the change"; pausing the ad set "for 7 days or longer"; every edit "has some effect on delivery"; editing during learning resets learning "and delay[s] our delivery system's ability to optimize"; frequent budget changes "can cause an ad set to re-enter the learning phase"; with Advantage+ campaign budget, ad sets "won't re-enter the learning phase as budget is distributed" but changing the campaign budget "might cause multiple ad sets" to re-enter; edit only with "reason to believe that the edit should improve performance." The article states Meta gives no percentage and the 20% figure is "a rule of thumb, not Meta's documentation." Article 27 Jun 2026, updated 1 Oct 2026 - [Bach.ai, what resets the learning phase](https://www.wittelsbach.ai/blog/what-resets-meta-learning-phase-safe-vs-clock-restart-edits)
- [VENDOR - author claim, not Meta] Switching off one ad inside an ad set, renaming, changing end dates are "not on Meta's list"; overnight/weekend ad-set pauses don't reset, two-week pauses do (inference from the 7-day wording) - [Bach.ai](https://www.wittelsbach.ai/blog/what-resets-meta-learning-phase-safe-vs-clock-restart-edits)
- [VENDOR] The 20% budget threshold traces to third-party blogs (WordStream, Niblin), not Meta; some guides advise raising budgets 20-30% at a time - [search summary of WordStream and others](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-learning-phase); [Mockingbird](https://www.mockingbirdhouse.com/learning/campaign-structure/what-is-the-learning-phase)
- [PRACTITIONER] Oct 2025: if an ad set is classified by Meta as "high performing" (Meta notifies you), the budget can be raised "quite a bit" without re-triggering learning; learning is "just a label" - [Jon Loomer podcast, 15 Oct 2025](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/the-learning-phase-is-just-a-label/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Adding ads to an existing ad set resets how Meta has learned to distribute budget among those ads, hence learning. For bigger changes (optimization event, bid strategy, placement tests) duplicate into a new ad set/campaign rather than editing a working one - [Jon Loomer, edits and learning phase](https://www.jonloomer.com/facebook-ads-edits-learning-phase/)
- [PRACTITIONER] "Do not touch a Facebook ad that's working" - the learning phase is when results are least stable - [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/do-not-touch-a-facebook-ad-thats-working/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Avoid constant manual budget changes; they usually make delivery less efficient - [Jon Loomer, Breakdown Effect](https://www.jonloomer.com/breakdown-effect/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Value rules let you shift bids by age, gender, placement, device without excluding anyone (example: 55+ spend share 70% -> 17%) - [Jon Loomer, 8 Essential Breakdowns](https://www.jonloomer.com/essential-breakdowns-meta-ads-manager/)

### Inferences
- Proposed "cost of change" ladder for the analyst (my synthesis from the Meta list above; cost = learning reset + data loss):
  0. No-cost (outside Meta): faster/better WhatsApp replies, qualifying questions in the chat, logging relevance correctly. No learning impact.
  1. Very low: pause the single weakest ad within an ad set (not on Meta's list) - but only once that ad has a real sample; with 4 carousels and ~2 conversations there is usually no evidence to pick one.
  2. Low / uncertain: small budget move between the two ad sets (ad set budgets, not campaign budget). Meta: magnitude-dependent; community heuristic: stay under ~20%. Example: warm 25 -> 30 ILS is +20%; new 50 -> 45 is -10%.
  3. Medium: add a new ad or edit an existing ad's creative/text/welcome message (creative change -> that ad set re-enters learning). Safer variant: new ad set or duplicate.
  4. High: targeting change in an ad set (age, gender, exclusions, adding/removing custom audience, mobile-only) -> learning reset for that ad set.
  5. Highest: change optimization event / objective / offer -> effectively a new campaign.
- With ~2 conversations/5 days the ad sets will not exit learning anyway (already known: 50 events/7 days is unrealistic), so a "learning reset" here costs mostly the few days of delivery history and stability, not a lost "learned" state. Still, each reset makes the next comparison noisier - so bundle changes into one decision point rather than drip-feeding them.
- The welcome message / message template is part of the ad creative in click-to-WhatsApp ads, so editing it is likely a creative edit (not verified on Meta's page).

### Gaps
- No Meta source found stating whether pausing an individual AD (vs ad set) triggers learning; the "not on the list" claim is from a vendor and Loomer's reading.
- No independent practitioner source found that explicitly ranks "what to change first" by cost; the ladder above is synthesis.
- Whether Meta's 2026 placement-control test affects Engagement/messages campaigns is unconfirmed (Loomer: Sales and Leads only).

## 5. Overlap between the warm custom-audience ad set and the Advantage+ audience ad set

### Takeaway
Meta does not make your ad sets bid against each other: when two of your ad sets are eligible for the same auction, it enters only the one with the highest total value. The real cost of overlap is under-delivery and slower learning for the losing ad set, and Advantage+ audience will naturally reach warm people (Loomer measured >35% of budget going to remarketing without any suggestions). So the "new audience" ad set very likely also spends on the people in the "knows Elad" ad set unless they are excluded in Audience Controls.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL via VENDOR] Meta "Understand auction overlap" (facebook.com/business/help/537699989762051): "we choose the ad with the highest total value to compete in the auction" and "This ensures that your ads will not bid against one another." Meta (quoted, page not named): overlap "can prevent an ad set from spending its full budget or achieving enough results to exit the learning phase", "typically results in less predictable performance", and is "not necessarily a bad thing." Meta's "About overlapping audiences" (facebook.com/business/help/1679591828938781): overlap "can lead to poor delivery of your ad sets." Meta lists high auction overlap among causes of "Learning limited" (facebook.com/business/help/269269737396981), and there is a "Campaign auction overlap" metric counting ad sets in one campaign that entered the same auction (facebook.com/business/help/957407462768373). Article 14 May 2026, updated 1 Oct 2026 - [Bach.ai, audience overlap](https://www.wittelsbach.ai/blog/audience-overlap-the-silent-roas-killer-in-meta-ads)

### Inferences
- In Elad's setup, the warm audience (~3,000-3,500) is a subset of what the Advantage+ ad set may reach. Per Meta's auction-overlap rule, when a warm person is eligible for both, only one ad set competes - so the 25 ILS warm ad set may under-spend or the 50 ILS ad set may be spending part of "new audience" money on people who already know Elad. Day-5 data cannot tell which.
- How to check without new tools: (1) whether the warm ad set spends its full 25 ILS/day; (2) frequency and reach in the warm ad set vs its audience size; (3) whether the relevant designer who messaged came via the "new" ad set but is actually someone who knows Elad (the conversation log can answer this better than Meta). The Audience Overlap tool can only compare the warm custom audience with the lookalike suggestion, not with what Advantage+ actually delivers to, and may refuse the warm audience if the 10,000 minimum applies.
- Options to present (owner decides): (a) leave as is - simplest, no reset, accept some overlap; (b) exclude the warm custom audience from the Advantage+ ad set via Audience Controls - makes "new audience" truly new and the two numbers readable, cost = learning reset of the larger ad set and possibly higher cost there (warm people are usually cheaper conversations); (c) merge into one Advantage+ ad set with the warm audience as a suggestion - fewer, larger ad sets, but loses the separate read on "knows Elad." Note the owner's standing rule: designers who already know Elad stay in the audience; option (b) keeps them in the warm ad set, so it doesn't violate that rule, but this should be confirmed with her.

- No source found showing how much of an Advantage+ ad set's spend goes to a warm audience for a messages objective (Loomer's 35% was a different setup).

## 6. Recommended routine / cadence and what pros deliberately don't react to

### Takeaway
Pros look daily but act rarely: daily checks are for breakage (delivery, disapprovals, budget pacing, WhatsApp replies), decisions are made at pre-set checkpoints defined by spend (multiples of target cost) rather than by calendar day, and they deliberately ignore day-to-day swings in average cost, single-conversation differences between segments, and early-learning results.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Watch daily trends rather than whole-period averages; avoid constant manual budget changes; take a hands-off approach - [Jon Loomer, Breakdown Effect](https://www.jonloomer.com/breakdown-effect/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Don't touch an ad that's working; learning phase is when results are least stable - [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/do-not-touch-a-facebook-ad-thats-working/)
- [OFFICIAL via VENDOR] Edit only with "reason to believe that the edit should improve performance"; every edit "has some effect on delivery" - [Bach.ai quoting Meta](https://www.wittelsbach.ai/blog/what-resets-meta-learning-phase-safe-vs-clock-restart-edits)
- [STATS] Decide checkpoints in advance; repeated peeking with stop-on-significance inflates false positives from 5% to 26.1% in Miller's example - [Evan Miller](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-run-an-ab-test.html)
- [VENDOR] Give a new campaign at least 4-7 days and a full week of stable spend before concluding; other guides say 3-4 weeks; a spend-based rule of ~3x acceptable CPA - [AdLibrary low-budget guide](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-low-budget-strategy); [GoTranscript small-budget transcript](https://gotranscript.com/public/optimizing-facebook-ad-campaigns-for-small-budgets-tips-and-strategies)
- [VENDOR] Weekly cadence example: review frequency and CTR weekly; wait 48-72 hours after a sudden drop across all ads before acting - [AdMakeAI](https://admakeai.com/blog/facebook-ad-creative-fatigue)
- [VENDOR] Value-rule/demographic changes: evaluate over at least 4 weeks - [Flighted](https://www.flighted.co/blog/the-meta-ads-breakdown-effect-explained)

### Inferences
- Proposed cadence for this campaign (synthesis):
  - Daily (report, no action unless broken): spend vs budget per ad set (is the warm ad set spending its 25?), conversations, relevant conversations from the log, ad rejections, frequency in the warm ad set, and whether every WhatsApp conversation got a fast reply.
  - Weekly / every ~200-300 ILS: cumulative cost per conversation and per relevant conversation with intervals (section 3); placement and device split once; any segment that has spent >= 3x target (120 ILS) with zero relevant conversations is flagged as "pattern" - but still read through the breakdown-effect lens before proposing an exclusion.
  - Milestone (500 ILS, day 14): owner's pre-set decision rule; age/gender/device vs log; overlap check; bundle any changes into one edit round.
- Deliberately do NOT react to: one-day cost spikes, single-conversation differences between placements/ads/ages, CTR differences on their own, "Learning" / "Learning limited" labels, and a segment with higher average cost but some results (breakdown effect).
- Every proposal shown to the owner should carry: the evidence level ("first sign" vs "pattern" per section 3), the edit type and its learning cost (section 4 ladder), and what will be checked afterwards and when.

### Gaps
- No direct published daily/weekly routine from Foxwell, Ben Heath, Depesh Mandalia or Common Thread Collective was retrieved in this session (searches returned only vendor content); the cadence above is synthesis from Loomer, Meta wording and statistics sources.
- No Hebrew/Israeli agency source with original guidance on these topics was found (Hebrew search returned only news sites and English Loomer pages).
